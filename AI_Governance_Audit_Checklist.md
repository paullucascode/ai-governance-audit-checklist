# AI Governance Audit Checklist

A practical checklist for internal auditors assessing AI governance, accountability, risk classification and controls across the AI lifecycle.

This checklist should be adapted to the nature, scale, complexity and impact of each AI use case. It is intended as a practical starting point, not a universal or exhaustive audit program.

## 1. AI inventory and ownership

- [ ] Has the organization identified the AI systems and AI-enabled tools currently in use?
- [ ] Does the inventory include generative AI, embedded vendor functionality and locally developed tools?
- [ ] Is each AI use case linked to a named business owner?
- [ ] Is there a named technology or system owner?
- [ ] Does the inventory record the system’s purpose, users, affected stakeholders, data sources and vendor?
- [ ] Are unapproved or informal uses of AI identified and addressed?
- [ ] Are active AI systems periodically reviewed and recertified?

## 2. Risk classification and approval

- [ ] Has each AI use case been classified according to its potential impact and risk?
- [ ] Does the classification consider the decisions influenced by the system?
- [ ] Does it consider whether individuals, vulnerable groups, legal rights, employment, finance, health or safety may be affected?
- [ ] Does it consider the sensitivity and permitted use of the data involved?
- [ ] Are approval requirements proportionate to the level of risk?
- [ ] Are higher-risk use cases subject to multidisciplinary review?
- [ ] Are exceptions documented, approved and time-limited?
- [ ] Is residual risk formally accepted by an appropriately authorized person?

## 3. Governance and accountability

- [ ] Are responsibilities for AI governance clearly documented?
- [ ] Are decision rights defined for business, technology, legal, privacy, compliance, security and risk functions?
- [ ] Is there an appropriate governance forum for material AI use cases?
- [ ] Are significant decisions, approvals and challenges recorded?
- [ ] Does management receive useful information about material AI risks, incidents and unresolved issues?
- [ ] Does the board or relevant committee receive proportionate reporting on significant AI exposure?
- [ ] Are escalation routes clear when risk exceeds approved thresholds?
- [ ] Does internal audit retain appropriate independence from AI design and implementation?

## 4. Policies and acceptable use

- [ ] Does the organization have an AI policy or equivalent governance requirements?
- [ ] Does the policy define permitted and prohibited uses?
- [ ] Does it address the use of public generative AI tools?
- [ ] Does it identify information that must not be entered into external AI systems?
- [ ] Does it define when human review is required?
- [ ] Does it establish documentation and evidence-retention expectations?
- [ ] Does it define incident reporting and escalation requirements?
- [ ] Are employees and contractors trained on the policy?
- [ ] Is compliance with the policy periodically monitored?

## 5. Data governance

- [ ] Is the data used by the AI system relevant and appropriate for its intended purpose?
- [ ] Is data obtained and used lawfully?
- [ ] Are data ownership and stewardship responsibilities clear?
- [ ] Is data lineage documented?
- [ ] Are data quality, completeness and accuracy assessed?
- [ ] Has management considered whether the data is sufficiently representative?
- [ ] Are material limitations and gaps documented?
- [ ] Are personal, confidential and privileged data appropriately protected?
- [ ] Are data-retention and deletion requirements defined?
- [ ] Are changes to important data sources controlled and monitored?

## 6. Model and system validation

- [ ] Are expected performance criteria defined before deployment?
- [ ] Has the system been tested against its intended use and operating environment?
- [ ] Are accuracy, reliability and robustness assessed using suitable methods?
- [ ] Are bias and fairness risks evaluated where relevant?
- [ ] Are material limitations, assumptions and thresholds documented?
- [ ] Are test results independently reviewed or challenged?
- [ ] Are failed tests, exceptions and remediation actions documented?
- [ ] Is explainability proportionate to the significance of the decisions involved?
- [ ] Are model, prompt, configuration and system changes subject to appropriate control?
- [ ] Is revalidation required after material changes?

## 7. Human oversight and transparency

- [ ] Is the level of human oversight appropriate to the risk of the use case?
- [ ] Do reviewers have enough information, time and expertise to challenge AI-generated recommendations?
- [ ] Can reviewers override, suspend or reject the system’s output?
- [ ] Is evidence of meaningful human review retained where appropriate?
- [ ] Has management assessed the risk of automation bias or overreliance?
- [ ] Are users informed of important system limitations?
- [ ] Are affected parties given appropriate information about AI-supported decisions?
- [ ] Are review, appeal or correction mechanisms available where appropriate?
- [ ] Are material AI-generated outputs independently verified before consequential use?

## 8. Privacy, security and confidentiality

- [ ] Has a privacy assessment been completed where required?
- [ ] Are access rights based on business need and least privilege?
- [ ] Are prompts, outputs, logs and training data protected appropriately?
- [ ] Are confidential and personal data prevented from entering unapproved tools?
- [ ] Are interfaces, integrations and application programming interfaces securely configured?
- [ ] Has the system been assessed for relevant cyber threats and vulnerabilities?
- [ ] Are security testing and remediation documented?
- [ ] Are logs sufficient to support investigation and accountability?
- [ ] Are data breaches and AI-related security incidents included in response plans?

## 9. Third-party and vendor risk

- [ ] Was due diligence completed before selecting the vendor or service?
- [ ] Does due diligence cover data use, security, privacy, performance and system limitations?
- [ ] Are contractual responsibilities clearly defined?
- [ ] Do contracts address data ownership, retention, deletion and permitted secondary use?
- [ ] Are subcontractors and external processing arrangements understood?
- [ ] Does the organization receive appropriate notification of material system changes?
- [ ] Are service performance, incidents and control issues monitored?
- [ ] Does the organization have adequate audit, evidence and escalation rights?
- [ ] Is there a viable suspension, exit or continuity plan?
- [ ] Does management recognize that vendor use does not transfer organizational accountability?

## 10. Deployment and change management

- [ ] Was deployment formally approved?
- [ ] Were users trained before receiving access?
- [ ] Are operating procedures and responsibilities documented?
- [ ] Are production access and configuration changes controlled?
- [ ] Are significant model, data, vendor and use-case changes assessed before implementation?
- [ ] Are emergency changes documented and reviewed afterward?
- [ ] Are outdated systems or use cases formally retired?
- [ ] Are records, data and access appropriately handled when an AI system is retired?

## 11. Monitoring and incident management

- [ ] Are performance and risk indicators defined?
- [ ] Are thresholds established for investigation, escalation or suspension?
- [ ] Is the organization monitoring drift, errors, bias and adverse outcomes?
- [ ] Are user behavior and inappropriate reliance monitored where relevant?
- [ ] Are complaints and stakeholder feedback considered?
- [ ] Are AI incidents consistently identified, recorded and classified?
- [ ] Are root causes and broader control implications assessed?
- [ ] Are corrective actions assigned, tracked and validated?
- [ ] Can the organization restrict or suspend AI use when risk becomes unacceptable?
- [ ] Are lessons from incidents incorporated into governance and control improvements?

## 12. Internal audit approach

- [ ] Is the audit objective clearly linked to material AI risks?
- [ ] Is the scope proportionate to the use case, impact, data sensitivity and maturity?
- [ ] Are suitable audit criteria identified?
- [ ] Do the criteria include organizational policies, risk appetite and applicable requirements?
- [ ] Are recognized frameworks used where relevant?
- [ ] Does the audit assess both control design and operating effectiveness?
- [ ] Does testing go beyond inquiry and management representation?
- [ ] Does the evidence include policies, inventories, approvals, contracts, minutes, testing records, logs and incident reports?
- [ ] Are relevant business, technology, risk, legal, privacy, security and user perspectives considered?
- [ ] Do findings clearly connect criteria, condition, cause, impact and recommended action?
- [ ] Does reporting distinguish inherent risk, control effectiveness and residual risk?
- [ ] Are material decisions and unresolved exposures communicated clearly to senior management and the board?

## Minimum evidence to request

Internal auditors should consider requesting:

- AI inventory and use-case register
- Risk classifications and approval records
- AI policies, standards and procedures
- Governance committee terms of reference and minutes
- Data lineage and data-quality documentation
- Model or system testing and validation reports
- Bias, fairness, privacy and security assessments
- Vendor due-diligence files and contracts
- User training and access records
- Monitoring dashboards and exception reports
- Incident, complaint and remediation records
- Change-management and system-retirement records
- Board and executive reporting on AI risk

## Common warning signs

Potential warning signs include:

- No reliable inventory of AI use
- Unclear ownership or accountability
- Significant use of unapproved public AI tools
- Risk classifications unsupported by evidence
- Governance driven primarily by a vendor
- Human review that is merely ceremonial
- Testing based on assertion rather than documented evidence
- No monitoring after deployment
- Material changes implemented without reassessment
- Weak incident escalation or evidence preservation
- Board reporting that is either absent or excessively technical
- Repeated acceptance of unresolved risk without clear authority
